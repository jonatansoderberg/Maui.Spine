## 0. Ground rules learned the hard way

- **Compare against the sketch, never judge from memory.** Every round: render all states, put them next to the concept cells, look, change one thing, repeat. Zoom into the part you are fixing (eyes, mouth, hairline). Twenty rounds is normal for a character.
- **One detail at a time.** When the owner lists problems, fix and verify them one by one, each with a zoomed before/after.
- **When a capture looks wrong, check the capture before the model.** `SCNView.Snapshot()` (`shotview`) shows geometry and morphs but no bloom; a live-window crop (`shotlive`) shows bloom but can return stale morph states. A screenshot once lied about morphs not working.
- **Debug by elimination with colour coding**: retint materials (`rest` red, `under` blue, …) in a copy of the package, render, see which part is where it should not be. Render offline in Python (textured, normals as colours) to separate data bugs from renderer bugs.
- **Make failures visible**: the lab's status line shows `Last load failed: …`; read it (`dump`) before guessing.
- **Measure, then change.** Give every round a number before and after: loose components (`hairparts.py`), gaps and head-through-hair (`hairview.py`), scalp/core pixels inside a silhouette (`seethrough.py`), hair pixels round the eyes (`eyecheck.py`), flakes over the skin (`cheekcheck.py`), hairline roughness (`hairline.py`), the grey head inside the hair on the owner's review variant (`greyhead.py` + `greycount.py`), the hair's front outline past the photo's (`frontexcess.py`), triangles of skin through the hair (`facealpha.py` for cut faces, `holes.py` for missing ones). Three "fixes" in a row that leave the number unchanged mean the cause is somewhere else.
- **Geometry first, alpha second.** A view's mask cut strand by strand into a surface that does not follow that view's hair reads as flakes (the bob's side in front of the ear, P25–P35). Carve the volume to the views' silhouettes first (`hairenv.py`'s hull), then let the alpha trim tips. A side or back view cuts only skin it shows in the open: a small patch of skin enclosed by its hair is the gap between two of its strands, and cut into the envelope it was a hole showing the neck.
- **Check the staged frame's offset.** A mask built on the photo and applied to a staged frame that pads it (mvstage.py puts the 1254 px photo at (106, 90) in a 1452 px square) sits a hundred pixels off; `photo_offset()` finds it by matching a patch. The misplaced face mask cost two rounds of "the hole in the bangs".
- **A groove's far wall faces away from every camera.** Use the unsigned view cosine for colour (a sheet shows both sides); clip it only for cuts.
- **Hidden is per chart, at the chart's resolution.** A coarse 1 cm hidden test misses locks overhanging the side by a few millimetres; bake a depth map per chart and call a face hidden where under 60 % of its samples are outermost (`HAIR_CHART_VISIBLE`). A face may only use a chart where it is visible, not foreshortened under 0.75 of its best (own normal) and faced by its smoothed normal; the island vote may only move it into such a chart.
- **An envelope cannot carry strand-level tips; cards can.** Twelve rounds (R33-R44, #542) cut the envelope by the views' strands, tapered toward the outline, on the outermost surface only, with the rim rebuilt as a card: one row of tapered tips at the nape, but slivers at every other angle, since the envelope does not follow the views' strands in depth. Keep the coarse (closed, smoothed) masks on an envelope and draw the tips with hair cards (`Tools/haircards.py`, rounds C1-C15): per view a ribbon of cards hangs from the envelope's silhouette in that view (the envelope built a little inside the outline where it is ragged, `hairenv.py inset=`), tilted along the hair's surface, textured by projecting the view with its hair mask as alpha, so the ends are the view's own tapered strands. The dead ends are in the #542 changelog and in `~/.cache/spine-avatar-lab/kairo/tools/experiments/tapered-tips-R33-R44/README.txt`.
- **The owner's own scalp pictures beat every rule for the skin under the hair.** Export the lab's views of layer 1 (the bald head with the scalp tinted) at `size 1200` (front, both profiles, back, the crown with `head 0 -35`), let the owner paint the roots and the hairline on them, fit them back by silhouette (`Tools/scalpfit.py`, `SCALP_VIEWS` in the generator) and sample them per point (`scalp_views()`: the union of the views' root masks, the colour from the views that show hair, only views facing the point at cosine 0.15-0.5 or better). Lessons (C16-C30, #542): the ear window and the under-grid photo rule must yield to the pictures; feed the depth buffers the bald model only (the envelope's vertices hid the scalp cell by cell: a checkerboard); the root mask settles to skin or roots (`SCALP_SETTLE`: averaged over the strands zone it baked a translucent band); the strands over the skin at the hairline are a BLEND overlay from the pictures (`hairline_strands()`), each face given to the view that draws hair on it; the pictures' skin is a lit render, lighter than the photo, so never project their skin (a halo round the hairline); a grazing view maps its silhouette fringe onto centimetres of surface (a streak down the neck). The lab's camera is the package's framing (`lab_project()`; the crop camera plus a silhouette fit agrees within 10 px for the turntable views).
- **Review the layers one at a time.** The owner numbered the hair's layers (1 scalp, 2 root cap, 3 hairline skirt, 4 envelope, 5 cards); `solo.py hide=` makes a package with any set hidden in seconds (the session's `layers.sh`), and the owner judged each in the lab before the next came in. Hide, never rebuild, to show a subset; and show nothing with the skin hidden (the inner shells and eyeballs show through). Flat-colour the rest (`flat=`) to show one layer alone. A geometric cut (faces within N mm of the head, `dropnear.py`) previews the same way, in seconds per threshold, before it goes into the generator (`HAIR_HEAD_GAP`: the owner picked 15 mm of 8/12/15/20).
- **A card must face its own view, from the right side.** Lessons from C1-C15: (1) a stretch of silhouette whose rim recedes along the view's axis (the nape's corner seen from the side) gives a card whose plane faces the perpendicular view, textured with pixels smeared along it: a rod under the nape; drop those stretches by the rim's 3D direction on a depth smoothed over 12 px and a 40 px baseline (the nape's edge wobbles by centimetres and a short baseline reads it as receding). (2) The silhouette from a profile runs under the near side onto the far side's bottom edge where the near side is shorter: keep only rims on the view's own half and within 3 cm of the nearest rim nearby. (3) A corner between two views is inset by both; weight each view's inset by the voxel's horizontal direction against the view's axis. (4) Each stretch goes to the view whose surface above the rim faces it best (the envelope's smoothed normals); a card seen edge-on from another view is only a line if its alpha has strand gaps, so fade the alpha out in the dark gaps between the view's strands past the rim. (5) Find which view's card an artefact is before theorising: dump the views (`KAIRO_CARDS_DUMP`), run `haircards.ribbon()` offline and print every piece's rim in the model frame, or split the `cards` primitive by view into packages and hide all but one (`solo.py hide=cards_right,…`). (6) The views' world units are Pixal3D's (the head about 1 unit, a view px about 0.00075); lengths written in metres came out 2.5 x short.
- **Texels are shared where a chart stacks faces.** In a box chart the outermost face wins the texel; a cut face in front cuts the face behind it too, and a visible face behind shows a triangle of skin. `hair_charts` redraws small groups of cut faces uncut; and a face dropped from a closed surface leaves the same triangle (`fill_small_holes`).
- **An artefact that survives changes to one layer is not in that layer.** Hide the layer (`solo.py hide=…`) or colour-code the materials (`solo.py flat=…`) and look again: a staircase along the hairline survived four hair rounds and was the face grid against the skull; fragments "in the hair" were the envelope's inner shell seen through the eye holes; a bald patch came from a face rule, not the hair.
- **The owner watches the lab window live.** A diagnostic package (hidden or flat materials) in the app bundle, or a half-finished round, is what they see. Render experiments in a burst and end every chain script by copying the last good package back (`cp … Avatar\ Lab.app/Contents/Resources/Avatars/kairo.spineavatar`), then reload (`start.sh`; `load kairo`): a `cp` alone does not change what is shown.
- **Do not turn a rule off to see what happens** on a model the owner is looking at. Every rule in `kairo_scan.py` has a comment saying what it guards against; a rule turned off brings that artefact back (the face-mask drop rule: inner shell through the eyes, slab flakes over the cheek).
- **Verify a mark at the owner's own view before saying it is fixed.** Their screenshot comes from the lab window with the head following the cursor; find the camera yaw and head pose by hair-silhouette IoU against it (the session's `viewmatch.py`: captures at a few yaws × `HEAD="yaw pitch"`; the marked forehead view was camera -30, head pitch 15), capture the candidate there, diff the hair masks (removed pixels in clusters, with coordinates) and crop the marked spots side by side. The "three slivers" of #542 were reported fixed from a piece count alone and were still there: they were strips of the forehead's slab 15–25 mm over the face grid, joined to the main hair, while the loose pieces the scrap rule took sat at the jaw. A strip can look loose and still share vertices with the body; `piecesplit.py` colours each welded piece to see what a piece is (the envelope's inner shell was 2 914 faces hidden inside the hair).
- **Judge a hair rule in the lab's own views, in software.** `lab_project` reproduces the lab's camera exactly (the projected hair's bbox matched the capture to the pixel), so outline and coverage rules run on the lab's views inside the generator: the outline opening (`HAIR_OUTLINE_OPEN`, a tongue under the bob 33 mm off the head that no gap or piece rule reached) and the window filling (`lab_hair_windows`: skull showing inside the hair's outline). Two traps: hair behind the head is not cover (test every face against a head depth buffer, else windows vanish), and coverage must honour the texture's alpha (16 sub-triangles per cut face). Preview on the package first (`silopen.py`, `uncut.py` in the session tools), and keep a preview GLB under 32 MiB with its sparse accessors' buffer views intact, or the lab silently shows another avatar.
- **Edit a hair texture texel by texel, never by sub-triangle.** The windows pass may un-cut whole faces (hair over hair is harmless), but a cut along an edge against the skin must follow the smoothed outline exactly: cutting the 16 sub-triangles of a face whose centres fell in a spike opened 2.5 mm specks elsewhere and left the teeth. Map each texel of a candidate face to its 3D point by barycentric coordinates in the uv triangle, project it with `lab_project`, and change only the front-most hair's texels (a hair depth buffer, 5 mm) that land in the spike or the notch. Measure the owner's view before and after with the same opening (`spikes()` in verify-final.sh): a pass that does not lower that number did not work, whatever the log says.
- **A defect seen on the grey review head may be the review mode.** With every material but the hair flat white, each alpha edge over the head renders as a white line (the mip-averaged alpha straddles the mask cutoff and the white head shows through). Before chasing a rim or a halo, capture the real package at the same view; over the dark roots the same edge is invisible (#542, the filled windows).
- **Teeth along a cut are geometry; the texture cannot round them.** Three passes failed in one night (#542): opening the outline per lab view and cutting texels (new teeth from the next view, since twelve views share one texture), cutting a view's small blobs (their backs stayed as chips), relaxing the mesh's boundary chains and cutting beyond the curve (chain vertices moved up to 42 mm, 85 000 texels gone). The owner's measure never dropped. Cut the mesh itself along smooth curves (Blender's knife/boolean through `bmcp.py`, or a mesh knife in the generator) and keep alpha for strand tips only. Before any of it, run the owner's measure on a package preview: a pass that does not lower it in the owner's own view is not a pass.
- **Edits by script must assert their anchors.** A multi-replacement script that fails on the last anchor writes nothing; check the grep count of the new text before starting a build on it.
- **Docs, changelog, commits in English**; conversation with the owner in Swedish. Stage explicit paths only (never `git add -A`). Work in the worktree, never check out in the main checkout.
- **Never copy third-party characters** (OpenAI's Dots, Clippy …): make your own in that spirit, and say so.

## 2. Pick the approach

| Want | Approach | Example | Tool |
|---|---|---|---|
| A stylised 3D character from a sketch (robot, mascot, plush, house) | Procedural GLB: SDF shapes sampled to meshes, morph targets for the face, clips for motion | **Nova**, **Puff/Mochi/Sprig/Bean/Home** | `Tools/nova.py`, `Tools/plush_avatars.py` |

| A ready-made GLB (CC0) | Package it; map its morphs/clips to poses | **Robot Expressive**, MPFB | `Tools/package_robot.py`, `Tools/package_mpfb.py` |

Procedural faces from parameters end up looking like Nintendo Miis (the owner said so of `face_styles.py`); for a real character use images (Kairo) or a hand-made model.

## 3. The package contract (what the bindings can say)

Manifest: `profile` (`character` needs all 7 states and 8 expressions, `ambient` does not), `representations[]` (`renderer` skia|native3d, `format` spine2d|sksl|glb, `capabilities`, owners for idle/blink/gaze), `states` (pose, transition, input/outputReactive, gaze mode), `expressions`, `speech` (canonical 15 visemes, `oculus15-v1`), `motions` (idle variants, gestures, blink interval), `themes.slots` (bindings `material:<i>` for GLB, `uniform:<name>` for SkSL, node paint paths for spine2d), posters.

GLB bindings (`bindings/native3d.json`), besides `poses` (lists of morph writes `{node, mesh, primitives, targetIndex, value}` or transform writes `{node, property, value}`) and `animations`:

| Key | Effect |
|---|---|
| `blink` | target(s) the scheduler drives 0→1→0; `suppressExpressionTargets` |
| `gaze` | `{node, rangeMeters}` slides a node; `{node, headRotation}` turns a bone; `{morphs: {mesh, primitives, left, right, up, down}}` drives look targets (full at ±12°); `turn: {node, factor}` turns a node toward a held look (pointer/tap) |
| `parameters.inputLevel` / `outputLevel` | level → transform or morph weight (`min`/`max`) — Nova's ear light, plush body pulse |
| `stateAnimations` | a looping clip per state (Nova `connect` = full spin, `listen` = ear pulse) |
| `springs: false` | no squash/lift/tilt (humans: the face must stay put) |
| `lighting: "portrait"` | soft front key, no coloured rim (skin) |
| `bloom: {intensity, threshold, radius}` | per-avatar glow (SceneKit threshold is luminance — blue needs high emission) |
| `muteBadge`, `headNode`, `speechMouthMesh`, `expressionMouthTargets` | as named |

SkSL: uniforms are listed in `Avatar.Core/AvatarShaderContract.cs` (time, `flow` phase, levels, `energy`, bands, mouth, `stateX`/`exprX` weights, gaze, look, springs, dark, accent, theme colours). Unknown uniforms fail validation. Animate with `flow`, not `iTime`, so speed changes stay smooth.

Build and run on the Mac (MAUI workloads live in `/usr/local/share/dotnet`):


Useful commands: `load <id>`, `state <idle|connecting|listening|thinking|speaking|interrupted|muted>`, `muted on|off`, `expr <name> <intensity> <seconds>`, `viseme <0-14>|off`, `blink <0-1>|off`, `level in|out <0-1>`, `fixture play` (speech with visemes), `gesture <name>`, `theme light|dark`, `renderer native|web`, `skia gpu|cpu`, `size <pt>`, `follow off` (the real mouse steers gaze otherwise), `lookat x y` (after `size`), `shotview <name>`, `shotlive <name>`, `shot <name>` (whole window), `dump`, `reset-peaks`, `bench`.

Scripts:
- `Tools/lab/states.sh <id> <concept.png> <out.png>` — all 7 states, light and dark, under the concept's cells (pass the cell x/y ranges for the sheet's layout).
- `REGION=x0,y0,x1,y1 Tools/lab/zoom.sh <id> <out.png> "cmd;cmd" …` — 640 pt renders, one crop per step (blink sequences, visemes, turns).
- `Tools/lab/phone.sh` — Release build with the harness, installs on the owner's iPhone (retries while it is unreachable; it must be unlocked and on the network). On the phone the harness reads `Library/harness.txt` (`xcrun devicectl device copy to … --domain-type appDataContainer --domain-identifier se.cosmomedia.spineavatarlab`).

Lab limits: the camera has `yaw` only (no pitch: views from above come from `hairview.py` offline); `size 1200` gives 2400 px captures; `shotview` is transparent (gaps to the room are alpha 0). A package can be swapped straight into the app bundle (`bin/Debug/net10.0-maccatalyst/maccatalyst-arm64/Avatar Lab.app/Contents/Resources/Avatars/`) without a dotnet build; `zoom.sh`/`earzoom.sh` never return inside a pipe, run them from a nohup script with a done-file. ## 5. Recipes

### Procedural 3D (Nova, plush)
1. Model with SDFs (`superellipsoid`, `ellipsoid`, `smin`/`smax`), sample along rays from a centre (`ray_surface`) on a UV sphere; star-shaped bodies only.
2. Faces of light (Nova): eyes and mouth are bands projected onto the visor; each shape is a morph target (rest pills, blink, happy arch, wide, closed, thinking-asymmetric, look L/R/U/D, mouth shapes for visemes). Keep rest geometry solid — glow layers at different depths read as ghosted eyes; bloom gives the glow.
3. Plush faces: eyes sunk into the surface; gaze as morph targets that re-project each eye onto the body at shifted spots (rotating about a centre sinks them).
4. Parts that animate are their own nodes (ears, connect rings, alert mark); show/hide by scale in poses; mic-off badge as a morph from a point.
5. Clips start and end at rest (they add onto poses). Default framing straight at the camera; turn the body with `gaze.turn`, not a rotated rest pose.

### From the owner's images (Kairo)
Inputs: a large, straight-on front image (neutral, eyes open, mouth closed), a **bald** version of it (same framing), profile/three-quarter/back and expression/blink sheets, and for the hair the owner's generated views (right/left profile, back, back three-quarters, crown; `~/.cache/spine-avatar-lab/kairo/assets-hair/`). Pixal3D runs locally (`~/.cache/spine-avatar-lab/pixal3d`, isolated venv, `pixal3d.cpp` built with Metal using `DEVELOPER_DIR=/Applications/Xcode-26.6.0.app/Contents/Developer` and its `SDKROOT`; Q8 weights: `models/pixal3d-sv` 8.4 GB single-view, `models/pixal3d-mv` the multiview flows with the shared files symlinked).

**Reconstructions** (each pixel-aligned to its own crop; a second run of a same-framed image is offset, fit `BALD_OY` by image matching):
- Haired front: `trellis-cli --bg-only` then `--sv-image cut.png --pixal3d-weights sv --res 1024 --webp off` (≈10 min to 2.5 h) → `kairo-s42.glb`.
- Bald front: the same → `bald/kairo-bald-s42.glb`: **the head**. The head never changes when hair goes on (owner's rule): the face grid shows the bald picture, nothing on it is filled or toned for the hair, and the grid's depth samples the head's faces only (sampled with the hair in, the forehead became a plate drawn to the bangs).
- Hair volume: `Tools/mvstage.py OUT front right back left` (in the repo since #542, with `mvfit.py` and `hairenv.py`) stages four matted views in one crop with Pixal3D's canonical cameras (front camera at -y, "right (azim 90)" at +x sees her LEFT side with the nose pointing left in the image, back at +y, left at -x, z up, distance 3.119, FOV 20°; mirrored sides put the face on the back of the head), then `trellis-cli --views OUT --models pixal3d-mv --res 1024 --webp off` (≈50 min). Check with `pixal3d/src/tools/silhouette_iou.py GLB OUT --res=256` (IoU 0.9-0.96 per view is good). Fit into the scan's frame with `tools/mvfit.py` by **front silhouette** (scale + x/y; depth by the noses); face ICP put the hair 27 px aside and 11 % small.

**Generator**: `KAIRO_HAIR=proj python Tools/kairo_scan.py <work-dir> <out-dir>` (≈1.5 min; `KAIRO_BALD=1` builds the head alone, `KAIRO_HAIR` unset the old scan-hair route). The parts that matter:
- `compose()`: the bald scan is skull, ears, neck, turtleneck (neck chain with harmonic weights, pivot at the ear canals); the hair source is the fitted multiview mesh's smooth **envelope** from `Tools/hairenv.py` (hair faces by texture colour and, near the front, by the photo's hair mask with the face mask excluded; the single-view scan's front strands thickened and added so the hairline follows the photo; voxelised at 2.5 mm, closed 8, carved by the side and back views' smoothed hair silhouettes (`hull_px` 8, inset 10 px where the outline is ragged, per view sector) and by the photo in the generator's camera (`front_px` 4, `inset_front` 8), marching cubes, Taubin-smoothed, 40 000 faces, front vertices pulled to 4 mm in front of the head; the shipped envelope is `res=0.0025 close=8 smooth=15 faces=40000 front_thick=4 pull=0.004 inset=10 inset_front=8`). Loose pieces (`loose_pieces`), hair inside the head's volume and hair 2-60 mm behind the face grid within the face are dropped. The lock ends can be **hair cards** (`Tools/haircards.py`, material `cards`, one 2048 px RGBA atlas, MASK; off by default since the owner dropped them, `KAIRO_CARDS=1` builds them, `KAIRO_CARDS_DEBUG=dir` writes per-view overlays, `KAIRO_CARDS_DUMP=file.npz` dumps the views for offline work). The root cap (`KAIRO_ROOTS=1`) and the hairline skirt (`KAIRO_SKIRT=1`) are off as well: the skull carries the owner's scalp pictures (`assets-scalp/`, `scalp_views()`), so the package is the head with that scalp plus the envelope.
- Texture: `hair_projector()` colours every point from the owner's views through Pixal3D's cameras (a depth buffer per view, weight (normal·camera)^4, each view's luminance matched to the photo's hair), fallback the palette's dark with strands where no view sees; baked by `hair_charts()` into six box charts plus a hairline chart (never a per-triangle atlas: at each triangle's UV jump the sampler takes the coarsest mip level and draws the mesh as grain), RGBA PNG, material MASK at 0.5 and nine tenths emissive like the front. Alpha 0 where a view sees a point squarely and unoccluded over skin (the front view also where the hair hugs the skin within 8 mm): the hairline is the photo's hair mask, the ear sits in a pocket.
- The face: a dense height-field grid over the bald picture (thin-plate spline through trusted depths), eye openings with eyeballs and round irises behind, lids and mouth as their own meshes with chains of morph targets; the face-mask drop rule removes hair faces over the face (keep it on: it costs the bangs' lock's thin end but guards the eyes and the cheek).
- Scalp under the hair is hair-coloured (a ray test against the hair volume plus the photo's envelope; the skull's front under the grid stays skin). `skin_bake`'s stray rule must spare it (it once wiped the whole scalp back to skin: beige between the strands for a day). Under the hair layer sits the **root cap** (`root_cap()`, material `roots`): the same skull faces lifted 0.5–2 mm, dark indigo root strands, coverage = that hair weight, alpha feathered; through any gap or cut in the hair the eye meets roots, on the owner's grey review head too (`KAIRO_ROOTS=0` leaves it out). The face grid's folded-back skirt is its own layer (`hairline_skirt()`, material `hairline`): the picture's skin where its rim lies on the photo's skin, roots where it lies under the photo's hair (as part of the grid it showed as skin triangles between the strands at the temples).
- Landmarks (eyes, lids, brows, mouth, jaw, collar) are measured by hand on the source image; re-measure for a new character.

### SkSL shader (Aurora Flow)
